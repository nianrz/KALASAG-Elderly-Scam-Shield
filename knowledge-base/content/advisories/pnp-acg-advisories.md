TYPES OF CYBERCRIME SCAMS

Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group (PNP ACG)

OVERPAYMENT SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Scammers overpay with counterfeit checks and request the difference to be wired back, then the check bounces. How it works: Victims receive payments larger than expected and are asked to refund the excess amount, only to find the original check was counterfeit. Warning signs: Protection:

JOB SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Fake job postings or recruitment offers used to obtain personal information or money from job seekers. How it works: Victims apply for jobs and are asked to pay for training or equipment, or their personal information is stolen. Warning signs: Protection:

IMPERSONATION SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Scammers pose as government officials, law enforcement, or company representatives to intimidate or trick victims into paying money or revealing information. How it works: Victims receive calls or messages from scammers claiming to be officials and demanding payment or personal information under threat. Warning signs: Protection:

FAKE ANTIVIRUS SOFTWARE
Posted on May 27, 2024

Pop-up ads or alerts claim your computer is infected and offer fake antivirus software that either does nothing or installs malware. How it works: Victims receive alarming pop-ups and are tricked into purchasing or downloading fake antivirus software. Warning signs: Protection:

CHARITY SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Scammers pose as charity representatives to solicit donations, often exploiting disasters or holidays. How it works: Scammers create fake charities or impersonate real ones to solicit donations that they pocket. Warning signs: Protection:

LOTTERY AND SWEEPSTAKES SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Victims are informed they've won a large prize but must pay fees or taxes upfront to claim it. How it works: Scammers notify victims of a fake lottery win and ask for payment of taxes or fees to release the prize. Warning signs: Protection:

ROMANCE SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Scammers create fake profiles on dating sites to form relationships and manipulate victims into sending money or sharing personal information. How it works: The scammer builds a relationship with the victim online, gaining their trust before asking for money under various pretenses. Warning signs: Protection:

INVESTMENT SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Fraudulent investment opportunities promising high returns with little risk, often involving Ponzi or pyramid schemes. How it works: Scammers use convincing websites, fake testimonials, and fabricated track records to lure victims into investing money. Warning signs: Protection:

ONLINE SHOPPING SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Fake online stores or fraudulent listings that trick consumers into paying for goods or services that are never delivered. How it works: Victims make purchases on fake or fraudulent websites and either receive counterfeit goods or nothing at all. Warning signs: Protection:

IDENTITY THEFT
Posted on May 27, 2024

Stealing personal information to commit fraud, such as opening new accounts or making unauthorized purchases. How it works: Attackers gather personal data through various means (e.g., phishing, data breaches) and use it to impersonate the victim. Warning signs: Protection:

TECH SUPPORT SCAMS
Posted on May 27, 2024

Scammers impersonate tech support representatives to gain access to computers and steal information or money. How it works: Victims receive unsolicited calls or pop-ups claiming their computer is infected and offering tech support services that either do nothing or install malware. Warning signs: Protection:

BUSINESS EMAIL COMPROMISE (BEC)
Posted on May 27, 2024

A scam targeting businesses by compromising or spoofing email accounts of executives to initiate fraudulent transactions. How it works: Attackers gain access to a company email account or create a similar one to trick employees into transferring money or sensitive data. Warning signs: Protection:
